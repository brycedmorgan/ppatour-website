The APP Tour, 2020–2026

Fewer events. Smaller fields.
Every year, in every market.

The APP publishes a registered-player count on whichever platform hosts its events. That makes their amateur business fully observable from the outside — and we pulled six seasons of it, event by event. Their field peaked in 2023. Through late July of 2026 they have registered fewer players than a single PPA Tour weekend.

PPA Tour assessment

Why the calendar is shrinking

Everything above is measured. What follows is our read as the operator with the most U.S. event weekends in this sport. We run this business; we know what each line of an event P&L costs.

A professional pickleball event stands on three revenue legs. Take any one away and the weekend loses money. Take two away and no amount of scheduling fixes it. On our read of the APP’s events, only one leg was ever carrying weight — and it is the one that just fell 73%.

1  Sponsorship

Sponsorship follows audience and broadcast guarantees. It is sold against attendance, reach and inventory an operator can prove. A calendar that loses six markets in one year is a calendar that is harder to sell nationally, and category deals price off the schedule you can commit to.

2  Gate

The APP’s stops are largely municipal parks and community complexes — Danny Cunniff Park, Sawyer Point, Spalding County. Those are excellent places to play and they are not ticketed stadium environments. Without a built venue there is no meaningful gate, no premium hospitality and no reserved seating to sell.

3  Registrations

Amateur entry fees are the one leg that pays cash up front, weeks before the event, and it is the leg we can measure from outside. It is down 73% year over year. On a $85 entry plus division fees, a field falling from 619 to 394 players removes a six-figure sum from a single weekend before a single cost is paid.

The pattern is a retreat, not a strategy

Operators do not remove New York City, Newport Beach and their Florida season opener from a calendar because those markets are working. Those were their three largest fields. Dropping your biggest events is what it looks like when the event-level economics stop closing — you cut the weekends that need the most cash to stage. The 2026 calendar also has no U.S. open event at all before 25 March, which is a quarter of the year with no registration revenue coming in.

Where we think this goes

We expect the contraction to continue: more markets removed, more of the calendar shifted to international and licensed events where the APP carries less cost, and continued erosion of the amateur field that funds the rest. A tour cannot run indefinitely on one shrinking revenue leg. We would treat a further step down in 2027 — or a sale, merger or wind-down of the U.S. tour — as the most likely outcomes, and we are planning our own calendar and partner conversations on that basis. This is our judgment, not a statement about the APP’s finances, which are private.

05  ·  Method

Where every number came from

All four sources are public. Nothing here is modelled, estimated or extrapolated — each figure is the count the hosting platform published for that event.

  1. 2020 – mid-2023 — PickleballTournaments.com. Event pages recovered from the Internet Archive, where the page counter reads “Registered Players.” We take the latest post-event capture. Some pages were never archived, so these three seasons are floors, not totals — the real numbers are higher, which makes the decline from the 2023 peak larger, not smaller.
  2. June 2023 – October 2024 — PickleballTournaments.com. Pulled live from the tournament search filtered to APP-sanctioned events, where the platform reports “Completed — N players.”
  3. October 2024 – December 2025 — UTR Sports. The APP moved tournament operations to UTR in April 2024. Counts come from UTR’s public event API — registeredCount, unique players, for the APP Tour club. Division-level entries run roughly 1.8× higher and are deliberately not used.
  4. 2026 — Pickleball Den. The APP moved platforms again for 2026. Each event page publishes “Players Registered.” Events whose registration is still open are labelled as such; the Chicago figure of 379 was captured two days before registration closed.
  5. PPA Tour figures come from the same PickleballTournaments.com “Completed — N players” metric as the APP’s own PT-era numbers, so the head-to-head is a like-for-like comparison of the same definition.
  6. Excluded on purpose: APP Asia and international stops, NPL events, and pro-only invitationals with no amateur draw — none of them compete for U.S. amateur entry. Collegiate series stops report teams rather than players and are excluded from every total.
